The repository keeps full benchmark tables, hardware metadata, raw JSON, and offline verification scripts in [docs/benchmarks.md](https://github.com/ShaulLavo/ghostty-webgpu/blob/main/docs/benchmarks.md). This page summarizes what was measured, what won, and where `ghostty-webgpu` still loses.

## November 2026 five-way comparison

Measured 2026-11-24 with `ghostty-webgpu` 0.3.34 against xterm.js 6.0.0 (WebGL addon 0.19.0), ghostty-web 0.4.0, Wraith (`wraithterm`) 0.6.1, and Restty 0.3.0 in headed Chromium 153 on two machines: a Linux desktop (Wayland, RTX 3060 Ti, DPR 1) and an Apple M1 MacBook (AC power, ANGLE Metal, DPR 2). Every library received identical bytes, a 100x30 grid, JetBrains Mono 14 px, and verified final screen text across 17 workloads at 1 and 16 terminals (34 cells per machine).

| Metric                                           | Linux PC                                     | Apple M1 Mac                               |
| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Whole-browser CPU vs. best competitor            | 26 wins, 5 even, 3 losses (median **0.71x**) | 24 wins, 9 even, 1 loss (median **0.77x**) |
| Mac kernel CPU energy (`RUSAGE_INFO_V6`)         | n/a                                          | 20 wins, 4 even, 10 losses                 |
| Write acknowledgement p95 (>10% & >0.5 ms loss)  | 4 losses (all heavy 16-terminal parsing)     | 0 losses                                   |
| Cold download (main entry, auto backend)         | **269.2 KiB** brotli                         | **269.2 KiB** brotli                       |
| Cold load to first paint (unthrottled / Fast 4G) | 80 ms / 823 ms                               | 101 ms / 861 ms                            |

Largest CPU leads appear on dense 16-terminal output (`rolling-unicode-logs` 0.47x PC / 0.50x Mac), `sparse-edits` at 16 terminals (0.45x PC / 0.60x Mac), `repeated-ascii` (0.20x PC / 0.35x Mac), and `write-queue-overload` (0.40x PC / 0.51x Mac; `ghostty-web` 0.4.0 crashes on overload).

## Where competitors still win

- **Download size and cold load:** xterm.js (84.1 KiB brotli) and ghostty-web (146.1 KiB brotli) are smaller and paint faster on Fast 4G than `ghostty-webgpu` (269.2 KiB brotli). Wraith (879.0 KiB) and Restty (559.3 KiB) are larger.
- **Glyph churn (1 terminal PC):** Restty WebGL2 uses less CPU (0.151 s vs. 0.222 s) because `ghostty-webgpu` currently rasterizes each new glyph twice to detect intrinsic color glyphs.
- **Full-screen redraw Mac energy:** Wraith WebGPU uses less Mac CPU energy on several 1-terminal and 16-terminal full-screen redraw workloads (`opentui-plasma`, `ntcharts-heatmap`, `synchronized-frames`).
- **16-terminal write acknowledgement p95 on PC:** Wraith WebGPU answers writes sooner on 16-terminal `ntcharts-heatmap` and `opentui-plasma` because it spawns one worker per terminal, while `TerminalWorkerPool` caps workers at 4 to save 35% to 60% whole-browser CPU.

## October 2026 paired Mac M1 ratios

The interactive charts on the [landing page](https://ghostty.shaulavo.dev/) come from the earlier paired Mac M1 run (`ghostty-webgpu` 0.3.21 vs. xterm.js 6.0.0, same renderer class, ABBA pairing). You can recompute and verify those numbers locally without a browser:

```sh
node docs/benchmarks/mac-m1-2026-10-10/verify.mjs
```

Read the full per-backend tables and methodology in [docs/benchmarks.md](https://github.com/ShaulLavo/ghostty-webgpu/blob/main/docs/benchmarks.md), or see how backend selection uses these findings in [Renderers and fallbacks](https://ghostty.shaulavo.dev/docs/guides/renderers/).
